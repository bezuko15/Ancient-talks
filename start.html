Start Here

New to the Church Fathers? You are in good company. This page explains who they were, why people still read them, and how to find your way around this site.

Who were the Church Fathers?

“Church Fathers” is the traditional name for the teachers, bishops and monks of the first centuries of Christianity whose writings shaped how the Church prayed, worshipped and explained its faith. The earliest, often called the Apostolic Fathers, wrote within living memory of the apostles: St Clement of Rome wrote to the church at Corinth around AD 96, and St Ignatius of Antioch wrote letters on his way to martyrdom in Rome early in the second century.

Later came writers such as St Irenaeus, St Athanasius, St Basil the Great, St Gregory of Nyssa, St John Chrysostom and St Augustine, who lived through persecution, the great councils and the end of the ancient world. In the East the tradition continued with figures like St Isaac the Syrian in the seventh century and St John of Damascus in the eighth.

The library also includes some much later Christian classics: Thomas à Kempis, Brother Lawrence and G.K. Chesterton. They are not Church Fathers, but they belong to the same long conversation.

Why read them?

They are some of the oldest Christian writing we have outside the New Testament, and they are honoured by Orthodox, Catholic and many Protestant Christians alike. Many of them are also simply good reading: practical, searching and often surprisingly direct about prayer, humility, money, suffering and love. You don’t need any special training to begin. Read slowly, a little at a time.

How to use this site

  • Library: full books you can read here for free or download as EPUB and plain text. Each one names the translation it uses.
  • Daily: one short passage a day, with a reading streak kept on your device.
  • Shorts: one-minute and five-minute readings.
  • Timeline and Authors: who lived when, with short biographies.
  • Watch: readings on the Ancient Talk YouTube channel.

Book pages remember where you stopped (only in your own browser, nothing is sent anywhere). They have a text-size control, and the ☾ button switches night mode on any page.

Four good first readings

About 20 minutes

The Didache

A short early Christian manual on the “Two Ways”, baptism, prayer and the Eucharist. Probably written in the late first or early second century.

An afternoon

On the Incarnation

St Athanasius explains why the Word of God became man. A short classic of Christian theology.

One book at a time

Confessions, Book I

St Augustine’s prayerful account of his own life. Start with Book I and read on as you like.

A modern voice

Orthodoxy

G.K. Chesterton’s lively 1908 account of how he came to believe. A good bridge for modern readers.
