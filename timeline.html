Timeline of the Fathers

From the first century to the twentieth: the writers in the Ancient Talk library, in order. “c.” marks an approximate date. The date on the left is roughly when each was active.

  1. Apostolic age

  2. c. 96

    St Clement of Rome

    d. c. 99

    Bishop of Rome; around AD 96 he wrote to the church at Corinth urging peace.

    First Epistle to the Corinthians

  3. c. 100

    The Didache

    late 1st or early 2nd c.

    An anonymous early church manual on the Christian way of life and worship.

    The Didache

  4. c. 108

    St Ignatius of Antioch

    d. c. 108

    Bishop of Antioch who wrote seven letters on his way to martyrdom in Rome.

    About IgnatiusEpistle to the Ephesians

  5. Second and third centuries

  6. c. 155

    St Justin Martyr

    c. 100 – c. 165

    Philosopher and apologist who explained the faith to the emperor; martyred in Rome.

    First Apology

  7. c. 180

    St Irenaeus of Lyons

    c. 130 – c. 202

    Bishop of Lyons, a hearer of Polycarp; defended the apostolic faith against Gnosticism.

    About IrenaeusAgainst Heresies

  8. c. 270

    The Desert Fathers

    3rd – 5th c.

    Monks and hermits of Egypt, beginning with St Anthony the Great (c. 251–356).

    About The Desert Fathers

  9. The age of the great councils

  10. c. 318

    St Athanasius

    c. 296 – 373

    Bishop of Alexandria and defender of the Nicene Creed, exiled five times.

    About AthanasiusOn the Incarnation

  11. c. 350

    St Cyril of Jerusalem

    c. 313 – 386

    Bishop of Jerusalem whose lectures prepared new Christians for baptism.

    Catechetical Lectures

  12. c. 360

    St Ephrem the Syrian

    c. 306 – 373

    Deacon and hymn-writer of Nisibis and Edessa, the “Harp of the Spirit.”

    About EphremHymns on the Nativity and The Pearl

  13. c. 375

    St Basil the Great

    c. 330 – 379

    Bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia; monastic founder and theologian of the Holy Spirit.

    On the Holy Spirit

  14. c. 380

    St Gregory the Theologian

    c. 329 – 390

    Gregory of Nazianzus, Cappadocian Father and defender of the Trinity at Constantinople.

    About GregoryThe Five Theological Orations

  15. c. 380

    St Gregory of Nyssa

    c. 335 – c. 395

    Basil’s younger brother, a Cappadocian Father and profound mystical writer.

    About GregoryOn the Making of Man

  16. c. 390

    St John Chrysostom

    c. 347 – 407

    Preacher of Antioch and Archbishop of Constantinople, called “Golden-Mouthed”.

    About John ChrysostomOn the Priesthood

  17. c. 400

    St Augustine of Hippo

    354 – 430

    North African bishop whose Confessions and City of God shaped Western Christianity.

    About AugustineConfessionsCity of GodOn Christian Doctrine

  18. The East after Rome

  19. c. 640

    St Maximus the Confessor

    c. 580 – 662

    Monk who confessed Christ’s two wills, divine and human, and died in exile.

    About Maximus

  20. 7th c.

    St Isaac the Syrian

    7th century

    Monk and briefly bishop of Nineveh in the Church of the East, beloved teacher of prayer and mercy.

    About Isaac the SyrianShorts

  21. c. 730

    St John of Damascus

    c. 675 – c. 749

    Monk near Jerusalem who summarised the faith of the Fathers and defended icons.

    Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith

  22. Later Christian classics

  23. c. 1420

    Thomas à Kempis

    c. 1380 – 1471

    Augustinian canon in the Netherlands, traditionally credited with The Imitation of Christ.

    About Thomas à KempisThe Imitation of Christ

  24. c. 1670

    Brother Lawrence

    c. 1614 – 1691

    Carmelite lay brother in Paris who practised the presence of God in the kitchen.

    The Practice of the Presence of God

  25. 1782

    The Philokalia

    compiled 1782

    Anthology of the Fathers on watchfulness and the prayer of the heart, published in Venice.

    About the Philokalia

  26. 1908

    G.K. Chesterton

    1874 – 1936

    English writer and apologist, author of Orthodoxy and The Everlasting Man.

    About G.K. ChestertonOrthodoxyThe Everlasting Man
