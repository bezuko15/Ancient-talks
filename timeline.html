Timeline of the Fathers

From the first century to the twentieth: the writers in the Ancient Talk library, in order. “c.” marks an approximate date. The date on the left is roughly when each was active.

  1. Apostolic age

  2. c. 96

    St Clement of Rome

    d. c. 99

    Bishop of Rome; around AD 96 he wrote to the church at Corinth urging peace.

    About ClementFirst Epistle to the Corinthians

  3. c. 100

    The Didache

    late 1st or early 2nd c.

    An anonymous early church manual on the Christian way of life and worship. The writer’s name is not known.

    About the DidacheThe Didache

  4. c. 108

    St Ignatius of Antioch

    d. c. 108

    Bishop of Antioch who wrote seven letters on his way to martyrdom in Rome. The Epistle to the Ephesians is one book here; the other six are together.

    About IgnatiusEpistle to the EphesiansSix Epistles

  5. Second and third centuries

  6. early 2nd c.

    St Polycarp of Smyrna

    d. c. 155

    Bishop of Smyrna and a disciple of the Apostle John. His letter to Philippi belongs to the early second century; the year is not fixed.

    About PolycarpEpistle to the Philippians

  7. c. 150–200

    The Epistle to Diognetus

    writer unknown

    An early Christian letter to a pagan named Diognetus. The date is only a cautious range, and the writer’s name has not survived.

    About the letterEpistle to Diognetus

  8. c. 155

    The Church at Smyrna

    soon after Polycarp’s death

    The church’s letter recounting the martyrdom of Bishop Polycarp. He died about 155; the letter was written soon afterwards.

    About the churchThe Martyrdom of Polycarp

  9. c. 155

    St Justin Martyr

    c. 100 – c. 165

    Philosopher and apologist who explained the faith to the emperor, then wrote a shorter appeal to the Roman senate. Martyred in Rome.

    About JustinFirst ApologyThe Second Apology

  10. c. 176–180

    Athenagoras of Athens

    2nd century

    A Christian philosopher. The plea is addressed to Marcus Aurelius and Commodus, so it belongs to their joint rule. The treatise on the resurrection is from the same period; its exact year is not fixed.

    About AthenagorasA Plea for the ChristiansOn the Resurrection of the Dead

  11. c. 180

    St Irenaeus of Lyons

    c. 130 – c. 202

    Bishop of Lyons, a hearer of Polycarp; defended the apostolic faith against Gnosticism.

    About IrenaeusAgainst Heresies

  12. c. 180–183

    St Theophilus of Antioch

    d. c. 183

    Bishop of Antioch. The third book to Autolycus mentions the death of Marcus Aurelius (180), and Theophilus himself died about 183.

    About TheophilusTo Autolycus

  13. c. 251–252

    St Cyprian of Carthage

    d. 258

    Bishop and martyr, commemorated in the Orthodox Church. Both treatises belong to the years of the Decian persecution; 258 is the year of his death, not of these books.

    About CyprianOn the Unity of the ChurchOn the Lord’s Prayer

  14. c. 270

    The Desert Fathers

    3rd – 5th c.

    Monks and hermits of Egypt, beginning with St Anthony the Great (c. 251–356). Their collected sayings are not a separate book on this site; Antony’s life is under Athanasius.

    About the Desert FathersLife of Antony

  15. The age of the great councils

  16. c. 318

    St Athanasius

    c. 296 – 373

    Bishop of Alexandria and defender of the Nicene Creed, exiled five times. On the Incarnation is the early work; the Life of Antony is later.

    About AthanasiusOn the Incarnation

  17. c. 350

    St Cyril of Jerusalem

    c. 313 – 386

    Bishop of Jerusalem whose lectures prepared new Christians for baptism. They are usually placed around 350.

    About CyrilCatechetical Lectures

  18. c. 357–362

    Life of Antony

    written by St Athanasius

    The life of Antony of Egypt, written after Antony’s death in 356. The year within that short span is not fixed.

    About AthanasiusLife of Antony

  19. c. 360

    St Ephrem the Syrian

    c. 306 – 373

    Deacon and hymn-writer of Nisibis and Edessa, the “Harp of the Spirit.”

    About EphremHymns on the Nativity and The Pearl

  20. c. 370–380

    Exhortation to Theodore

    St John Chrysostom

    Two early letters calling Theodore back to the ascetic life, from before Chrysostom’s diaconate. The year is approximate.

    About ChrysostomExhortation to Theodore after His Fall

  21. c. 370

    The Hexaemeron

    St Basil the Great

    Nine Lenten homilies on the six days of creation. They are usually placed a little before On the Holy Spirit; the year is approximate.

    About BasilThe Hexaemeron

  22. c. 375

    On the Holy Spirit

    St Basil the Great, c. 330 – 379

    Bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia; monastic founder and theologian of the Holy Spirit.

    About BasilOn the Holy Spirit

  23. c. 379–380

    St Gregory of Nyssa

    c. 335 – c. 395

    Basil’s younger brother. On the Making of Man completes Basil’s homilies on creation after Basil’s death in 379. The dialogue with Macrina belongs to the same years.

    About GregoryOn the Making of ManOn the Soul and the Resurrection

  24. c. 380

    Letter to a Young Widow

    St John Chrysostom

    A letter of comfort written in his Antioch years. The year is approximate.

    About ChrysostomLetter to a Young Widow

  25. c. 380–382

    St Gregory the Theologian

    c. 329 – 390

    Gregory of Nazianzus. The Theological Orations belong to his time in Constantinople (380); On the Holy Lights is the Epiphany sermon of 381; the funeral oration for Basil was delivered about 382.

    About GregoryThe Five Theological OrationsOration on the Holy LightsFuneral Oration on St Basil

  26. c. 381–386

    On the Priesthood

    St John Chrysostom

    A dialogue on the dignity and burden of the priestly calling, usually dated to these years rather than to one year.

    About ChrysostomOn the Priesthood

  27. c. 385

    The Great Catechism

    St Gregory of Nyssa

    A short sum of Christian teaching for those who instruct others, from later in his life. The year is approximate.

    About GregoryThe Great Catechism

  28. 387

    Homilies on the Statues

    St John Chrysostom

    Twenty-one Lenten homilies preached at Antioch in 387, after the statues of the emperor were overthrown.

    About ChrysostomHomilies on the Statues

  29. c. 386–397

    Antioch homilies

    St John Chrysostom

    Instructions to Catechumens, and three homilies on resisting the devil. Both belong to his years as a presbyter in Antioch; neither has a fixed year.

    About ChrysostomInstructions to CatechumensHomilies on Resisting the Devil

  30. c. 396–426

    St Augustine of Hippo

    354 – 430

    North African bishop. Confessions is about 397–400. On Christian Doctrine was begun about 396 and finished about 426. The City of God was written from 413 to 426, after the sack of Rome.

    About AugustineConfessionsOn Christian DoctrineThe City of God

  31. c. 404–407

    No One Can Harm

    St John Chrysostom

    A short treatise written in exile near the end of his life and sent to the deaconess Olympias.

    About ChrysostomNo One Can Harm the Man Who Does Not Injure Himself

  32. c. 420–429

    St John Cassian

    c. 360 – c. 435

    The Institutes of the monastic life, written at Marseilles. He is commemorated in the Orthodox Church. Gibson’s 1894 translation omits Book VI and says so.

    About CassianThe Institutes

  33. The East after Rome

  34. c. 640

    St Maximus the Confessor

    c. 580 – 662

    Monk who confessed Christ’s two wills, divine and human, and died in exile. No public-domain English of his works is on this site.

    About Maximus

  35. 7th c.

    St Isaac the Syrian

    7th century

    Monk and briefly bishop of Nineveh in the Church of the East, beloved teacher of prayer and mercy. His homilies are not a separate book here.

    About Isaac the SyrianA short reading

  36. c. 730

    St John of Damascus

    c. 675 – c. 749

    Monk near Jerusalem who summarised the faith of the Fathers and defended icons.

    About John of DamascusExact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith

  37. Later Christian classics

  38. c. 1420

    Thomas à Kempis

    c. 1380 – 1471

    Augustinian canon in the Netherlands, traditionally credited with The Imitation of Christ.

    About Thomas à KempisThe Imitation of Christ

  39. c. 1670

    Brother Lawrence

    c. 1614 – 1691

    Carmelite lay brother in Paris who practised the presence of God in the kitchen.

    About Brother LawrenceThe Practice of the Presence of God

  40. 1782

    The Philokalia

    compiled 1782

    Anthology of the Fathers on watchfulness and the prayer of the heart, published in Venice. The English translations are still under copyright.

    About the Philokalia

  41. 1905

    Heretics

    G.K. Chesterton

    Essays on the thinkers of his day, published in 1905.

    About G.K. ChestertonHeretics

  42. 1908

    Orthodoxy and Thursday

    G.K. Chesterton, 1874 – 1936

    Orthodoxy, his account of how he came to believe, and the novel The Man Who Was Thursday, both published in 1908.

    About G.K. ChestertonOrthodoxyThe Man Who Was Thursday

  43. 1925

    The Everlasting Man

    G.K. Chesterton

    His account of humanity and Christ, published in 1925.

    About G.K. ChestertonThe Everlasting Man
