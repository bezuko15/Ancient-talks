Authors

d. c. 108

St Ignatius of Antioch

Bishop of Antioch, arrested and taken to Rome to be martyred. On the way he wrote seven letters to churches urging unity around the bishop and the Eucharist.

c. 130 – c. 202

St Irenaeus of Lyons

A disciple of Polycarp, who knew the Apostle John. As bishop of Lyons he wrote Against Heresies, defending the apostolic faith against Gnosticism.

c. 296 – 373

St Athanasius

Bishop of Alexandria and tireless defender of the Nicene Creed, exiled five times. His On the Incarnation remains one of the clearest accounts of why God became man.

c. 347 – 407

St John Chrysostom

Archbishop of Constantinople, called “Golden-Mouthed” for his preaching. His homilies on Scripture and the Divine Liturgy bearing his name are still in daily use.

c. 335 – c. 395

St Gregory of Nyssa

One of the Cappadocian Fathers with his brother Basil the Great and Gregory of Nazianzus; a profound mystical theologian of the soul's endless ascent into God.

354 – 430

St Augustine of Hippo

North African bishop, converted after a restless youth in 386. His Confessions and City of God shaped Western Christianity more than almost any other writings.

3rd – 5th c.

The Desert Fathers

Hermits and monks of the Egyptian desert such as St Anthony the Great and Abba Poemen. Their short sayings on prayer, humility and repentance are timeless.

7th c.

St Isaac the Syrian

Isaac of Nineveh, a monk and briefly bishop of the Church of the East, who withdrew to solitude. His Ascetical Homilies on prayer, tears and God's mercy are beloved across East and West.

c. 1380 – 1471

Thomas à Kempis

An Augustinian canon in the Netherlands, generally credited with The Imitation of Christ, a gentle guide to inward life and humility.

1874 – 1936

G.K. Chesterton

English journalist, novelist and apologist known for his paradox and good humour. Author of Orthodoxy, The Everlasting Man and the Father Brown stories; he entered the Catholic Church in 1922.
