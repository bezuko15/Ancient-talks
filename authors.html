Authors

d. c. 155

St Polycarp of Smyrna

Bishop of Smyrna and a disciple of the Apostle John. His letter to Philippi and the account of his martyrdom are among the earliest Christian writings outside the New Testament.

Epistle to the Philippians The Martyrdom of Polycarp

2nd century

Athenagoras of Athens

A Christian philosopher who wrote a plea for the Christians to the emperors and a treatise on the resurrection of the body.

A Plea for the Christians On the Resurrection of the Dead

d. c. 183

St Theophilus of Antioch

Bishop of Antioch. His three books to Autolycus defend the Christian faith to a pagan friend.

To Autolycus

d. 258

St Cyprian of Carthage

Bishop of Carthage and a martyr. He is commemorated in the Orthodox Church. These are his treatises on the unity of the Church and on the Lord’s Prayer, from a third-century Latin writer, not a later Roman Catholic author.

On the Unity of the Church On the Lord’s Prayer

c. 360 – c. 435

St John Cassian

A monk who carried the teaching of the Egyptian desert to the West. He is commemorated in the Orthodox Church. Gibson’s 1894 translation omits Book VI and says so.

The Institutes

d. c. 108

St Ignatius of Antioch

Bishop of Antioch, arrested and taken to Rome to be martyred. On the way he wrote seven letters to churches urging unity around the bishop and the Eucharist.

c. 130 – c. 202

St Irenaeus of Lyons

A disciple of Polycarp, who knew the Apostle John. As bishop of Lyons he wrote Against Heresies, defending the apostolic faith against Gnosticism.

c. 296 – 373

St Athanasius

Bishop of Alexandria and tireless defender of the Nicene Creed, exiled five times. His On the Incarnation remains one of the clearest accounts of why God became man.

c. 306 – 373

St Ephrem the Syrian

Deacon of Nisibis and later Edessa, and the greatest poet of the Syriac-speaking Church, called the “Harp of the Spirit.” He taught the faith in hymns sung by choirs; the Lenten prayer that bears his name is said throughout Great Lent.

Hymns on the Nativity and The PearlThe Prayer of St Ephrem

c. 347 – 407

St John Chrysostom

Archbishop of Constantinople, called “Golden-Mouthed” for his preaching. His homilies on Scripture and the Divine Liturgy bearing his name are still in daily use.

On the PriesthoodHomilies on the StatuesNo One Can Harm the Man Who Does Not Injure HimselfPrayers

c. 329 – 390

St Gregory the Theologian

Gregory of Nazianzus, friend of St Basil the Great and one of the Cappadocian Fathers. As bishop in Constantinople he preached the Five Theological Orations in defence of the Holy Trinity, which earned him the title “the Theologian.”

The Five Theological Orations

c. 335 – c. 395

St Gregory of Nyssa

One of the Cappadocian Fathers with his brother Basil the Great and Gregory of Nazianzus; a profound mystical theologian of the soul's endless ascent into God.

354 – 430

St Augustine of Hippo

North African bishop, converted after a restless youth in 386. His Confessions and City of God shaped Western Christianity more than almost any other writings.

3rd – 5th c.

The Desert Fathers

Hermits and monks of the Egyptian desert such as St Anthony the Great and Abba Poemen. Their short sayings on prayer, humility and repentance are timeless.

7th c.

St Isaac the Syrian

Isaac of Nineveh, a monk and briefly bishop of the Church of the East, who withdrew to solitude. His Ascetical Homilies on prayer, tears and God's mercy are beloved across East and West.

c. 580 – 662

St Maximus the Confessor

Byzantine monk and theologian who defended the truth that Christ has a human will as well as a divine will. For this confession he was mutilated and died in exile; the Sixth Ecumenical Council (680–681) upheld his teaching. No public-domain English translation of his works exists yet, so we link to the original Greek.

Greek and Latin text (Migne, PG 90) ↗

compiled 1782

The Philokalia

An anthology of writings on watchfulness and the prayer of the heart by Fathers from the 4th to the 15th century, compiled by St Nikodemos of the Holy Mountain and St Makarios of Corinth. The English translations are still under copyright, so we link to the first Greek edition.

Greek edition, Venice 1782 ↗The Jesus Prayer

c. 1380 – 1471

Thomas à Kempis

An Augustinian canon in the Netherlands, generally credited with The Imitation of Christ, a gentle guide to inward life and humility.

1874 – 1936

G.K. Chesterton

English journalist, novelist and apologist known for his paradox and good humour. Author of Orthodoxy, The Everlasting Man and the Father Brown stories; he entered the Catholic Church in 1922.
